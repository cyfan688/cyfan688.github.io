Research motivation

I was initially interested in large language models (LLMs) because of their rapid development and remarkable potential. When I was a teenager, I often encountered difficult questions or became curious about phenomena that I could not explain. Because I was somewhat shy about asking teachers questions, I usually tried to find answers on my own. LLM chatbots appeared to be valuable tools because they could provide immediate explanations and help me explore unfamiliar topics.

Over time, however, I also began to recognize their limitations. LLMs generate responses based primarily on the results learned from existing data. They can organize and combine knowledge without understanding what it actually means,Similarly, receiving a fluent answer does not necessarily mean that the user understands the underlying concepts or mechanisms. People may rely on LLMs therefore create an illusion.

Nevertheless, I believe that LLMs definitely can help reduce educational inequality. I grew up in Huangshan, a city with relatively limited economic and educational resources. People there work hard while often receiving fewer opportunities, and I deeply admire their diligence and optimism toward life. For motivated students who lack access to strong educational support, AI can serve as a patient and accessible tutor.

My interest in reinforcement learning began when I watched a video on Bilibili in which a developer used RL to accomplish a task that had previously seemed out of reach. Watching the agent learn through repeated trial and error and eventually succeed left a strong impression on me. I later explored applications of RL in robotics, games, healthcare, industrial control, and the training and alignment of LLMs. More recently, I have also seen developers train RL agents to play online games and livestream their behavior. Watching these agents learn, fail, and gradually improve is both technically fascinating and genuinely entertaining.

So I think, maybe it really is different, maybe it really can surpass the limits of humans.

I am particularly interested in offline reinforcement learning because it addresses situations in which real-world experimentation is expensive, risky, or impractical. Instead of repeatedly collecting new samples, an offline RL agent must learn from previously collected experience. Such settings are common in industrial production, healthcare, and other real-world domains where data collection can be difficult or costly. Therefore, I believe that offline RL addresses an important problem with substantial practical value.
